# Budget Categories User Guide

## Understanding Your 5 Budget Categories

Your personal finance app now tracks spending across **5 comprehensive categories** to give you better control over your money.

---

## The 5 Categories Explained

### 1. 🔵 **Needs** (Essential Expenses)
**What it includes:**
- Housing (rent/mortgage)
- Food & Groceries
- Transportation
- Utilities (electricity, water, internet)
- Healthcare
- Insurance

**Recommended allocation:** 50-60% of income

**Why it matters:** These are non-negotiable expenses required for basic living. Keeping this under control ensures financial stability.

---

### 2. 🟠 **Wants** (Lifestyle Expenses)
**What it includes:**
- Entertainment
- Dining out
- Shopping/Clothing
- Subscriptions (streaming, gym)
- Hobbies
- Travel

**Recommended allocation:** 20-30% of income

**Why it matters:** These enhance your quality of life but can be adjusted if needed. This is where you have the most flexibility.

---

### 3. 🟢 **Savings** (Emergency Fund & Short-term Goals)
**What it includes:**
- Emergency fund contributions
- Short-term savings goals
- Rainy day fund
- Vacation savings
- Large purchase savings

**Recommended allocation:** 10-15% of income

**Why it matters:** Protects you from unexpected expenses and helps achieve near-term goals without going into debt.

---

### 4. 🟣 **Growth** (Long-term Wealth Building)
**What it includes:**
- Retirement contributions (401k, IRA)
- Investment accounts
- Stock market investments
- Real estate investments
- Business investments
- Education/skill development

**Recommended allocation:** 10-20% of income

**Why it matters:** Builds long-term wealth and financial independence. The earlier you start, the more compound growth works in your favor.

---

### 5. ⚪ **Buffer** (Miscellaneous & Uncategorized)
**What it includes:**
- Miscellaneous expenses
- One-time purchases
- Gifts
- Donations
- Unexpected small expenses
- Items that don't fit other categories

**Recommended allocation:** 0-5% of income

**Why it matters:** Provides flexibility for expenses that don't fit neatly into other categories. Keeps your budget realistic.

---

## How to Set Up Your Categories

### Step 1: Go to Expense Limits
Navigate to the **Expense Limits** page from the main menu.

### Step 2: Set Your Monthly Income
Enter your total monthly income at the top of the page.

### Step 3: Assign Categories
For each expense type, you'll see:
- **Category:** Which of the 5 categories it belongs to
- **Recommended %:** Suggested allocation
- **Your %:** Your custom allocation

### Step 4: Balance to 100%
Adjust the percentages until the running total equals **100%**. The app will show:
- ✅ Green checkmark when balanced
- ⚠️ Warning when not balanced

### Step 5: Save
Click **Save Limits** to apply your budget.

## Tips for Success

### 1. **Start with Recommended Percentages**
The app suggests allocations based on financial best practices. Adjust based on your situation.

### 2. **Track Regularly**
Check your Monthly Expense page weekly to see how you're tracking against your budget.

### 3. **Adjust as Needed**
Life changes! Update your allocations when:
- You get a raise
- Your rent changes
- You have new financial goals
- Your priorities shift

### 4. **Prioritize Growth**
Even small amounts in the Growth category compound over time. Start with 5% if 10-20% feels too high.

### 5. **Use Buffer Wisely**
If your Buffer category is consistently high, review those expenses and see if they should be categorized differently.

## Common Questions

### Q: What if I can't save 10-15%?
**A:** Start with what you can, even 2-3%. The habit is more important than the amount initially.

### Q: Should debt payments go in Needs?
**A:** Yes, minimum debt payments are Needs. Extra payments can go in Growth (if paying off high-interest debt) or Savings.

### Q: Can I have 0% in Buffer?
**A:** Yes! If all your expenses fit neatly into the other 4 categories, you don't need Buffer.

### Q: What if my Needs are more than 60%?
**A:** This is common in high cost-of-living areas. Look for ways to reduce Wants and still maintain some Savings and Growth.

### Q: How is this different from 50/30/20?
**A:** The traditional 50/30/20 rule combines Savings and Growth into one 20% category. Separating them helps you balance short-term security (Savings) with long-term wealth (Growth).

## Adjusting Your Budget

### When to Increase Needs
- Moving to a more expensive area
- Health issues requiring more medical care
- Family size increases

### When to Decrease Wants
- Trying to pay off debt faster
- Saving for a major purchase
- Building emergency fund

### When to Increase Growth
- Debt-free or low debt
- Emergency fund fully funded
- Approaching retirement age
- Received a raise

### When to Use Buffer
- Holiday season (gifts)
- Irregular expenses
- Testing new expense categories
- Transitional periods
